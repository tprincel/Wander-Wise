About Virupaksha Temple

At the heart of Hampi lies an ancient temple steeped in legend and boundless magnificence. A temple with a rich history that spans over 1000 years. A temple with a pinnacle that seems to touch the heavens. Welcome to the Virupaksha Temple.

The Virupaksha temple is a pilgrimage site that holds immense religious significance for Hindus, drawing devotees from far and wide. It is dedicated to Lord Shiva in his form as Virupaksha, meaning "eyes without form."

From humble origins as a modest shrine in the 7th century, it blossomed into a magnificent complex under the reign of the Vijayanagara rulers, who added their own touches to the temple, making this structure truly one-of-a-kind.

Legendary Origins

  • Divine marriage site of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati
  • Parvati's penance in Hemakuta Hills overlooking temple site
  • Blessed union ground sanctified by divine marriage
  • Auspicious wedding venue believed to be blessed by deities
  • Ancient name Pampa - the sacred realm where Parvati was born

Historical Significance

  • Over 1000 years of continuous worship and history
  • 7th century origins as a modest shrine
  • Vijayanagara Empire expansion and enhancements
  • Continuous operation as active place of worship
  • Architectural evolution spanning multiple dynasties

Architectural Features

  • Towering gopuram reaching towards heavens
  • Ornate mandapam for auspicious weddings
  • Sacred sanctum housing Virupaksha deity
  • Intricate carvings depicting mythological scenes
  • Vijayanagara style with Dravidian influences

Spiritual Significance

  • Major Hindu pilgrimage site attracting global devotees
  • Virupaksha form representing "eyes without form"
  • Divine marriage site with eternal spiritual energy
  • Active worship continuing ancient traditions
  • Sacred ceremonies and festivals throughout year
