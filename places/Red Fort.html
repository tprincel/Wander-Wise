About Red Fort

Step back in time and immerse yourself in the grandeur of the Red Fort (Lal Qila), Delhi's crown jewel and a UNESCO World Heritage site. A symbol of power and beauty, the Red Fort was the heart of Shahjahanabad (present-day Old Delhi), the bustling new capital built by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan.

In 1639, Shah Jahan decided to move his court from Agra to Delhi and ordered the Red Fort to be built as his majestic new home. It served as the residence of Mughal emperors for almost 200 years, witnessing the rise and fall of one of history's greatest empires.

This architectural marvel, named for its towering red sandstone walls, along with the historic Salimgarh Fort, creates a stunning and expansive heritage site that continues to captivate visitors from around the world.

Architectural Features

  • Red sandstone facade giving the fort its iconic name
  • Intricate carvings showcasing Mughal craftsmanship
  • Majestic arches and breathtaking marble structures
  • Private apartments with elegant pavilions
  • Nahr-i-Behisht - 'Stream of Paradise' water channel

Historical Significance

  • Mughal imperial residence for nearly 200 years
  • Shah Jahan's capital shift from Agra to Delhi
  • Architect Ustad Ahmad Lahori - creator of Taj Mahal
  • Pinnacle of Mughal artistry and refinement
  • Indian independence symbol - Prime Minister's address

Architectural Style

  • Islamic traditions with unique pavilion designs
  • Persian, Timurid, and Hindu influences blended
  • Innovative Mughal style influencing regional architecture
  • Picturesque gardens enhancing royal ambiance
  • Red Fort Complex with Salimgarh Fort integration

Visitor Experience

  • Light and sound show depicting Mughal history
  • Museum exhibitions showcasing imperial artifacts
  • Architectural photography opportunities
  • Historical immersion in Mughal era grandeur
  • Cultural events and national celebrations
