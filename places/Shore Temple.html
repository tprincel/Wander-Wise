About Shore Temple

The splendour of South Indian architecture radiates brightly in this shore. The majesty and glory of the Mamallapuram Shore Temple is one for all ages. No trip to Tamil Nadu is complete without a visit to the historic town of Mahabalipuram or Mamallapuram, where monuments, temples, and UNESCO World Heritage Sites transport you back in time.

Believed to be built during the reign of the old Pallavan king Rajasimha/Narasimhavarman II, the structure is one of the oldest temples of significance in South India. Until recently, the shore temple was buried under sand, preserving its ancient beauty for modern discovery.

The temple complex consists of two sanctums – one dedicated to Lord Shiva and the other to Lord Vishnu, reflecting the true essence of Dravidian architecture in its purest form.

Architectural Features

  • Built of cut stones and blocks of granite
  • Pyramidal Kutina-type tower with stepped stories
  • Cupola and finial topping the structure
  • Two sanctums dedicated to Shiva and Vishnu
  • Pure Dravidian style reflecting Pallava artistry

Historical Significance

  • Built 8th century CE under Pallava dynasty
  • One of Seven Pagodas mentioned by Marco Polo
  • Recently excavated after being buried in sand
  • Oldest significant temple in South India
  • UNESCO designation recognizing global importance

The Seven Pagodas Legend

  • Marco Polo's travels identified seven temples
  • Shore Temple believed to be last of seven pagodas
  • 2004 tsunami exposure revealed collapsed temple
  • Six temples believed to remain submerged in sea
  • Series of temples built along coastline

Spiritual & Cultural Significance

  • Dual dedication to both Shiva and Vishnu
  • Ancient meditation site offering peace
  • Coastal location symbolizing spiritual connection
  • Architectural masterpiece inspiring generations
  • Living history connecting past to present
