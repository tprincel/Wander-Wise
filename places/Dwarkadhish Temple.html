DWARKADHISH TEMPLE

Basic Information

Location

Dwarka, Gujarat, India

Deity

Lord Krishna (Dwarkadhish)

Significance

One of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites

About Dwarkadhish Temple

The main draw for tourists coming to Dwarka, the Dwarkadhish Temple (Jagat Mandir), is believed to have been established more than 2500 years ago by Lord Krishna's great grandson, Vajranabh. The ancient temple has been renovated several times, especially leaving imprints of 16th and 19th centuries.

The temple stands on a small hill accessed by 50 plus steps, with heavily sculptured walls that cocoon the sanctum with the main Krishna idol. Around the complex lie other smaller shrines. The walls have intricately carved mythical characters and legends.

The impressive 43 m high spire is topped with a flag made from 52 yards of cloth that flutters in the soft breeze from the Arabian Sea behind the temple. There are two doors (swarg and moksh) for the entry and exit of the temple.

Architectural Features

  • 43-meter high spire dominating the skyline
  • 50+ steps leading to the hilltop temple
  • Intricately carved walls with mythical characters
  • Two ceremonial doors: Swarg Dwar and Moksh Dwar
  • 52-yard cloth flag fluttering atop the spire

Historical Significance

  • Established 2500+ years ago by Vajranabh (Krishna's grandson)
  • Original structure destroyed by Mahmud Begada in 1472
  • Rebuilt in 15th-16th century with subsequent renovations
  • Honored by Adi Shankaracharya in 8th century
  • Dwarka's seventh avatar - city submerged six times before

Spiritual Significance

  • One of Char Dham - four holiest Hindu pilgrimage sites
  • Kingdom of Lord Krishna who built the city of Dwarka
  • Scenic location at cusp of Gomti River and Arabian Sea
  • Sudama Setu bridge connecting temple to beach
  • Major pilgrimage center attracting devotees worldwide
